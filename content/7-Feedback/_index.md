### Overall impressions

What I remember most about FCAJ is building a product that actually runs on AWS instead of isolated exercises. Ask First forced me to solve problems that are not in any syllabus: an OCR model dropping Vietnamese letters, a Lambda that must load models before any user arrives, and a shared environment that needs someone to keep permissions and secrets in order. That is where I learned the most.

I also got used to working like a development team: every change goes through a PR, with review, measurements and an eval gate on CI.

### Satisfaction level

**Satisfied: 4/5.**

- Satisfied with the hands-on environment: real AWS usage, an office to work from, and events to attend.
- Events such as the Buildrathon Kickoff and Student Community Day let me meet practitioners and students from other universities.
- Not a full 5/5 because of guidance in the first weeks; it took me time to know what to focus on for the AI role.

### What could be improved

- A suggested learning path per role (AI, backend) for the first 2–3 weeks.
- A mid-term architecture review with a mentor, so teams catch weak design choices early.
- Clearer guidance on cost and permission management when teams set up their own AWS account.

### Would I recommend it to friends?

**Yes.** I would recommend FCAJ to software engineering students who want to work in cloud or AI, because:

- The program has you build a real product with a clear target user, not just theory.
- You work with AWS services in a real operating context: Lambda, S3, DynamoDB, Bedrock.
- Teamwork follows a PR, review and CI process similar to industry.

I would tell friends to prepare basic AWS and Git knowledge beforehand, so they do not lose the first weeks the way I did.
